Module 1 — The AIoT Revolution Learning Objective: Understand how IoT, AI, and AIoT relate and evolved

📖 ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE

From Connected Devices to Intelligent Systems

The Internet of Things (IoT) began as a vision of everyday objects connected to the internet — thermostats, meters, vehicles — sharing data. But data alone does not create intelligence. Artificial Intelligence of Things (AIoT) is the convergence of IoT infrastructure with AI and ML capabilities, enabling systems that not only collect data but understand it, learn from it and act on it autonomously.

🔌
IoT

Connects physical devices to the internet. Enables data collection and remote monitoring. Devices follow fixed rules. Example: A smart meter that reports electricity usage every 15 minutes.

🤖
AI / ML

Learns patterns from data, makes predictions and decisions. Works on historical datasets. Example: A model predicting energy demand for the next 24 hours.

⚡
AIoT

Combines both: real-time sensor data feeds AI models that make on-the-spot decisions and trigger actions. Example: A campus system that detects an unoccupied classroom and autonomously recommends switching off lights.

Evolution Timeline

1990s
Embedded Systems
Microcontrollers in appliances — no networking, no data sharing, fixed programs
2000s
Machine-to-Machine (M2M)
Industrial sensors communicating over proprietary networks. Centralised monitoring.
2010s
Internet of Things (IoT)
IP-connected devices, cloud dashboards, consumer smart home. Data at scale but mostly stored, not acted on intelligently.
2015+
AI-enabled IoT
Cloud ML on IoT data streams. Predictive maintenance, anomaly detection. AI runs in the cloud, not at the edge.
2020+
AIoT — Edge AI
ML inference moves to the edge device. TinyML on microcontrollers. Federated learning. Real-time intelligent decisions without cloud dependency.

IoT — Internet of Things

▼
The network of physical devices (sensors, actuators, appliances, vehicles) embedded with electronics, software and connectivity that enables them to collect and exchange data. IoT devices follow pre-programmed rules and thresholds. They do not learn from data.

AI — Artificial Intelligence

▼
A broad field concerned with building systems that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence: reasoning, problem-solving, perception, language understanding and learning. In the context of AIoT, AI usually refers to machine learning algorithms applied to sensor data.

ML — Machine Learning

▼
A subset of AI in which algorithms learn patterns from data rather than being explicitly programmed with rules. Types relevant to AIoT: Supervised learning (regression, classification), Unsupervised learning (clustering, anomaly detection), Reinforcement learning (control).

Edge AI / TinyML

▼
Running ML inference directly on the edge device (microcontroller, embedded processor, Raspberry Pi) rather than sending data to the cloud. Benefits: ultra-low latency, works offline, preserves data privacy, reduces bandwidth cost. TinyML specifically refers to ML models small enough to run on microcontrollers (e.g. ESP32, Cortex-M) with kilobytes of RAM.

AIoT — Artificial Intelligence of Things

▼
The convergence of IoT infrastructure (sensors, connectivity, embedded hardware) with AI and ML capabilities (learning, prediction, decision-making). AIoT enables systems to move beyond simple telemetry toward autonomous perception and action — sensing the world, understanding it, and responding intelligently.

Cloud Computing

▼
On-demand delivery of computing resources (servers, storage, databases, ML) over the internet. In IoT, the cloud provides scalable data storage, large-scale ML training and global device management. Limitation: requires internet connectivity and introduces latency.

Q1. Which statement best describes AIoT?

A. A standard for connecting devices to the internet
B. The convergence of IoT infrastructure with AI and ML for autonomous sensing and decision-making
C. A cloud computing framework for IoT dashboards
D. A type of wireless communication protocol
AIoT combines the connectivity and sensing capabilities of IoT with the learning and decision-making capabilities of AI/ML. Neither IoT alone nor AI alone constitutes AIoT.

Q2. What is the key advantage of Edge AI over cloud-only ML inference?

A. Edge AI is always more accurate than cloud ML
B. Edge AI requires no training data
C. Edge AI enables low-latency inference without internet dependency, preserving privacy
D. Edge AI eliminates the need for sensors
Edge AI runs inference locally — this means decisions can be made in milliseconds even without internet, and sensitive data never leaves the device. The trade-off is limited compute and memory on edge hardware.

Q3. A smart irrigation system that automatically turns on pumps based only on a soil moisture threshold (e.g. <40%) is an example of:

A. Machine learning inference
B. Rule-based automation
C. Unsupervised learning
D. Federated learning
A simple if-then threshold rule is rule-based automation, NOT machine learning. ML would involve training a model on historical data to predict optimal irrigation schedules. The portal always clearly distinguishes these two approaches.

Module 2 — End-to-End AIoT Architecture Click each layer to explore its responsibilities

📖 ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE

The AIoT stack is organised into six functional layers. Data flows upward from physical sensors to applications, while control decisions and commands flow back down. Click any layer to see its details.

Layer 6
📱 Application, Visualisation & Decision Layer
Grafana · Custom Dashboards · Mobile Apps · Actuator APIs
▼

Responsibilities: User interface, alert visualisation, closed-loop actuator control, reporting

Data In: Analytics outputs, recommendations, anomaly scores, predictions

Data Out: Display, alerts to users, actuator commands (e.g. turn fan ON), scheduled reports

Key design choices: Role-based access, alert thresholds, dashboard layout for projector use

Failure points: Misconfigured alerts causing alarm fatigue; actuator commands sent without safety checks

▲ Recommendations & Commands  |  ▼ Display & Actions
Layer 5
🤖 AI/ML Analytics Layer
scikit-learn · TensorFlow Lite · ONNX Runtime · Python
▼

Responsibilities: Pattern recognition, anomaly detection, classification, regression, prediction

Data In: Historical & streaming sensor data, feature vectors

Data Out: Predictions, anomaly scores, classifications, recommended actions with method labels

Common models: Isolation Forest (anomaly), Random Forest (classification), Linear Regression (trend), K-Means (clustering)

Important: Always label whether output comes from a threshold rule, statistical method, or trained ML model. Never mix them up.

▲ Analytics Queries  |  ▼ Predictions
Layer 4
☁️ Cloud & Data Platform Layer
AWS IoT Core · Azure IoT Hub · ThingsBoard · InfluxDB · Node-RED
▼

Responsibilities: Long-term time-series storage, global accessibility, device management, large-scale data ingestion

Data In: MQTT messages / HTTP POST from edge devices and gateways

Data Out: Historical data for ML training, dashboards, alerts, device management commands

Failure points: Internet dependency, cloud outages affecting availability, data ingestion lag

▲ Filtered Data  |  ▼ Cloud Commands
Layer 3
⚙️ Edge & Embedded Processing Layer
ESP32 · Raspberry Pi 4 · NVIDIA Jetson · Arduino (limited)
▼

Responsibilities: Local data filtering, aggregation, protocol bridging, edge inference (TinyML)

Data In: Raw sensor readings (GPIO, I2C, SPI, UART)

Data Out: Filtered readings, local alerts, MQTT/HTTP messages to cloud, actuator signals

Why here? Reduces bandwidth, enables offline operation, preserves privacy of raw sensor data, lowers cloud cost

Failure points: Limited RAM/compute for complex models; firmware bugs; power loss

▲ Raw Signals  |  ▼ Structured Messages
Layer 2
📡 Connectivity & Messaging Layer
Wi-Fi · BLE · Zigbee · LoRaWAN · MQTT · HTTP/HTTPS · CoAP
▼

Responsibilities: Reliable data transport, message queuing (MQTT broker), protocol translation (gateway)

Data In: Sensor readings and actuator commands from edge/cloud

Data Out: JSON/CBOR payloads delivered to MQTT topics or HTTP endpoints

Key protocol choice: Short-range/high-data → Wi-Fi; long-range/low-power → LoRaWAN; local mesh → Zigbee; low-power short-range → BLE

Failure points: Network congestion, packet loss, QoS mismatches, unencrypted communication

▲ Physical Phenomena  |  ▼ Digital Signals
Layer 1
🌡️ Physical / Sensing Layer
DHT22 · PIR · HC-SR04 · MQ-135 · Accelerometers · Actuators
▼

Responsibilities: Measure physical phenomena (temperature, motion, vibration), convert to electrical signals, actuate physical outputs

Data In: Physical environment (heat, light, gas concentration, motion, pressure)

Data Out: Analog voltages or digital signals (I2C, SPI, 1-Wire, PWM)

Design choices: Sensor placement, sampling rate (Nyquist theorem), supply voltage (3.3V vs 5V), power source (battery vs mains)

Failure points: Sensor drift over time (requires recalibration), electrical noise, corrosion, power loss, physical damage

💡 Data flow trace example: Temperature sensor reads 31.2°C → DHT22 outputs digital signal → ESP32 reads via GPIO → Applies moving average filter → Publishes to MQTT: campus/room101/temperature {"value":31.2,"unit":"°C"} → ThingsBoard ingests → Isolation Forest scores 0.78 → Dashboard alert: "Temperature elevated — recommend ventilation"

Module 6 — Edge Computing & Edge AI

📖 ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE — Metrics shown are conceptual, not measured benchmarks
⚙️ Edge Processing
  • Latency: Milliseconds (no round-trip)
  • Works offline — no internet needed
  • Data stays on device (privacy)
  • Bandwidth: Only sends summaries to cloud
  • Compute: Limited by MCU/SBC hardware
  • Best for: Safety-critical, real-time decisions
☁️ Cloud Processing
  • Latency: Hundreds of ms to seconds
  • Requires reliable internet connectivity
  • Raw data leaves device (privacy risk)
  • Unlimited scalable compute
  • ML training on large datasets
  • Best for: Historical analytics, large-scale ML, global dashboards

Microsoft's IoT gateway. Device-to-cloud telemetry, cloud-to-device commands. Pairs with Stream Analytics, Cosmos DB.

Open-source IoT platform. Free Community Edition. Rule chains, real-time dashboards, device management. Can self-host on Raspberry Pi.

🔴 Node-RED

Flow-based visual programming. Runs locally on Pi. Excellent for prototyping MQTT → dashboard pipelines. Free and open-source.

📉 InfluxDB

Time-series database purpose-built for IoT. Sub-second queries on millions of data points. Pairs with Grafana for visualisation.

📊 Grafana

Open-source dashboard. Connects to InfluxDB, PostgreSQL, Prometheus. Used for IoT monitoring dashboards worldwide.

📊 Regression Metrics
  • MAE — Mean Absolute Error: average magnitude of errors
  • RMSE — Root Mean Square Error: penalises large errors more
  • R² — Coefficient of determination: proportion of variance explained
🎯 Classification Metrics
  • Accuracy — Correct predictions / total (misleading with imbalanced classes)
  • Precision — True positives / (TP + FP) — avoid false alarms
  • Recall — True positives / (TP + FN) — avoid missed faults
  • F1-Score — Harmonic mean of precision and recall

Module 11 — AIoT Security, Privacy & Responsible Design

Security Measures
  • Device authentication (X.509 certificates, JWT tokens)
  • TLS/SSL encryption for all MQTT and HTTP traffic
  • Firmware signing and secure boot
  • Network segmentation — IoT devices on separate VLAN
  • Minimal exposed ports and services
  • Regular firmware updates and vulnerability patching
  • Never hardcode credentials — use secure key stores
Privacy & Responsible AI
  • Data minimisation — collect only what is needed
  • Consent and transparency with data subjects
  • Purpose limitation — don't repurpose data
  • Data retention policies — delete when no longer needed
  • Explainable AI — label every decision with its method
  • Human oversight for safety-critical decisions
  • Bias awareness in training data and model outputs
⚠️This module raises awareness of threats for defensive purposes only. No offensive attack methods, malware techniques or exploitation details are discussed.
